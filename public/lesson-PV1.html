Reading & Writing Numbers to 1,000,000

PV1 · Numbers to 1,000,000 · NZ Year 6

Every digit has a home — its place value column
Big numbers up to 1,000,000 have 7 columns. Each column is worth 10 times more than the one to its right. The digit in that column tells you how many of that value you have.

Reading a number — use the split trick
Split the number at the comma into two groups. Read the thousands group first, say "thousand", then read the rest.
The split trick
405,200 → split → 405 | 200
"four hundred and five" + "thousand" + "two hundred"
= four hundred and five thousand, two hundred
300,000→three hundred thousand
600,050→six hundred thousand and fifty
800,808→eight hundred thousand, eight hundred and eight
1,000,000→one million
Writing digits from words — zeros hold the place!
Find the thousands group first, then the rest. Fill each column with its digit. If a column isn't mentioned, write a zero — never leave it blank!
Watch out for missing columns
"six hundred thousand and fifty" — no hundreds mentioned → hundreds column = 0
6 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 5 | 0 → 600,050 ✓

"four hundred and five thousand, two hundred"
4 | 0 | 5 | 2 | 0 | 0 → 405,200 ✓
Without zeros, digits slide into the wrong columns!
60050 ≠ 600,050 — the zeros are what make the number six hundred thousand, not sixty thousand.
