Applied Word Problems

AS6 · Addition & Subtraction · NZ Year 6

What is a word problem?
A word problem hides a maths calculation inside a real-life story. Your job is to read carefully, work out what you know, decide what operation to use, and calculate the answer.
The 4-step RUSC method
R — Read the problem all the way through.
U — Underline the key numbers and the question.
S — Select the operation (+ or −).
C — Calculate and write a sentence answer.
Always write a sentence!
Don't just write "15,725". Write: "The school now has 15,725 books." This shows you understood the question and answered it fully.
Step 1 — Spotting Addition keywords ➕
These words usually mean you need to add:
altogether in total combined how many … now? receives / gets more sum increased by total production
"Two schools raise money. School A raises $23,450 and School B raises $31,675. How much money did they raise altogether?"

Keyword: altogether → Add
$23,450 + $31,675 = $55,125
Step 2 — Spotting Subtraction keywords ➖
These words usually mean you need to subtract:
how many left? how much further? moved away has completed difference reduced by remaining how many fewer?
"A marathon track is 42,195 meters. A runner has completed 18,750 meters. How much further do they need to run?"

Keyword: how much further → Subtract
42,195 − 18,750 = 23,445 m
Step 3 — Setting up the calculation
Write down only the numbers you need. Label them clearly. Then calculate using a written method — column addition or subtraction.
Example — multi-part problem:
"A factory produces 125,450 items in January and 234,275 items in February. What is the total production?"

Known: Jan = 125,450   Feb = 234,275
Operation: total → Add
  125,450
+234,275
───────
= 359,725
Check your answer makes sense!
Estimate first: 125,000 + 234,000 ≈ 359,000. The answer 359,725 is close — looks right! If your answer is wildly different from your estimate, check your working.
Watch out — tricky wording!
Some problems sound like one operation but need another. Always re-read the question at the end — that's what you must answer.
Tricky example:
"A school receives a donation of 3,275 more books."
→ "Receives more" = add.

"12,450 people moved away."
→ "Moved away" = subtract.
Two-number trap:
Sometimes both numbers are in the problem but you only need one of them. Read the question carefully to know which numbers to use!
Final check — the 3 Cs
✅ Calculation — did you use the right operation?
✅ Check — does the estimate match?
✅ Complete sentence — did you write a full answer?
