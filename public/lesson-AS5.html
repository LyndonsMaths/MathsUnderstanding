Use Estimation to Check Reasonableness

AS5 · Addition & Subtraction · NZ Year 6

What is estimation and why does it matter?
An estimate is a quick, approximate answer. We use estimation to predict what an answer should be before we calculate, then check afterwards that our answer makes sense — that it is reasonable.
The two-step habit
Step 1 — Before calculating: Round the numbers and estimate the answer.
Step 2 — After calculating: Compare your real answer to your estimate. Are they close? If not, you may have made an error!
Real world example
You calculate 47,832 + 31,456 = 89,288. Is that reasonable?
Quick check: 50,000 + 30,000 = 80,000. Your answer is close to 80,000 — looks good! ✓
Step 1 — Rounding to estimate 🔄
Round each number to its leading digit (the first digit). Then add or subtract the rounded numbers. This gives a quick estimate to compare against.
Round to the nearest 10,000:
47,832 → 50,000  (rounds up — digit after 4 is 7)
31,456 → 30,000  (rounds down — digit after 3 is 1)
Estimate: 50,000 + 30,000 = 80,000
Round to the nearest 100,000:
499,999 → 500,000
500,001 → 500,000
Estimate: 500,000 + 500,000 = 1,000,000
Rounding rule: look at the digit to the right
If it is 5 or more → round UP the target digit.
If it is 4 or less → keep the target digit the same (round down).
Replace all digits to the right with zeros.
Step 2 — Is the answer reasonable? 🤔
After you have your estimate, compare it to the calculated answer. A reasonable answer is close to the estimate. If they are very different, something has probably gone wrong.
Example — reasonable ✓
Problem: 88,654 − 41,222
Estimate: 90,000 − 40,000 = 50,000
Calculated: 47,432
Is 47,432 close to 50,000? Yes — reasonable ✓
Example — not reasonable ✗
A student says: 245,000 + 755,000 = 900,000
Estimate: 250,000 + 750,000 = 1,000,000
Is 900,000 close to 1,000,000? It's 100,000 off — something went wrong! Not reasonable ✗
How close is "close enough"?
There is no fixed rule — it depends on the numbers. Generally, if your calculated answer is within 10% of your estimate, it is reasonable. A large difference (like being off by 100,000 on a 1,000,000 problem) signals an error.
Using inverse operations to check ⟲
Another way to check reasonableness is to use the inverse (opposite) operation. If you added, subtract. If you subtracted, add back. The result should give you back your starting number.
Check an addition with subtraction:
305,421 + 194,579 = 500,000
Check: 500,000 − 194,579 = 305,421 ✓
The answer checks out!
Check a subtraction with addition:
88,654 − 41,222 = 47,432
Check: 47,432 + 41,222 = 88,654 ✓
Three ways to check reasonableness
1️⃣ Estimate first — round and approximate before calculating.
2️⃣ Compare estimate to answer — are they close?
3️⃣ Inverse operation — work backwards to verify.
Rounding Practice — round to the nearest 10,000 or 100,000
